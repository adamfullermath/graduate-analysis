Example 3.3.3.

Let \(f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}\text{.}\) Define a function \(\gamma\) on intervals \((a,b]\subseteq \bR\) by taking the Riemann integral of \(f\) over \((a,b].\) That is,
\begin{equation*} \gamma((a,b]) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx. \end{equation*}
One can show that \(\gamma\) is a premeasure on the semiring of intervals of the form \((a,b]\text{.}\) Thus, by the Carathéodory-Hahn Theorem 2.3.16, \(\gamma\) extends to a Borel measure on \(\bR.\) In statistics and probabilty theoery, the measure \(\gamma\) is called the Gaussian distribution, or the normal distribution. The cumulative distribution function of the Gaussian distribution is usually denoted by \(\Phi\text{.}\) By definition \(\Phi\) is given by
\begin{equation*} \Phi(x) = \gamma((-\infty,x]) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^x e^\frac{-x^2}{2}. \end{equation*}
Note that \(\Phi\) is continuous on every closed interval \([a,b]\) in \(\mathbb{R}\text{.}\) Further,
\begin{equation*} \Phi'(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{\frac{-x^2}{2}}. \end{equation*}
