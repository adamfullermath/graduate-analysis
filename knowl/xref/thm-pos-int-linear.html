Theorem 6.1.20.

Let \((X,\mathcal{F},\mu)\) be a measure space, let \(f\) and \(g\) be non-negative measurable functions, and let \(\alpha\text{,}\) \(\beta\) be non-negative real-numbers. Then
\begin{equation*} \int_X(\alpha f + \beta g)\,d\mu = \alpha \int_X f\,d\mu + \beta \int_X g\,d\mu. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

By the Simple Approximation Theorem there are increasing sequences of non-negative simple functions \((\phi_n)_n\) and \((\psi_n)_n\) such that \((\phi_n)_n\) converges to \(f\) pointwise and \((\psi_n)_n\) converges to \(g\) pointwise. Thus \((\alpha\phi_n + \beta\psi_n)_n\) is an increasing sequence of non-negative simple functions which converge pointwise to \(\alpha f +\beta g\text{.}\) Using the Monotone Covergence Theorem and Theorem 6.1.3 and the Monotone Convergence Theorem again we get
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \int_X (\alpha f + \beta g)\,d\mu &= \lim_n \int_X(\alpha \phi_n + \beta \psi_n)\,d\mu \\ &= \alpha \lim_n\int_X \phi_n\,d\mu + \beta \lim_n \int_X \psi_n \,d\mu \\ &= \alpha \int_X f\,d\mu + \beta \int_X g\, d\mu. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
