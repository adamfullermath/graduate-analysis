Proposition 5.1.15.

Let \((X,\mathcal{F})\) be a measurable space, let \(f\) be a real-valued measurable function on \(X\text{,}\) and let \(\varphi \colon \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\) be a continuous function. Then \(\varphi \circ f\) is continuous.
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Take any open set \(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}\text{,}\) and let \(V = \varphi^{-1}(U)\text{.}\) Then \(V\) is an open subset of \(\mathbb{R}\text{,}\) since \(\varphi\) is continuous. By, Proposition 5.1.4, \(f^{-1}(V)\) is a measurable set. Thus,
\begin{equation*} (f\circ \varphi)^{-1}(U) = f^{-1}(V) \end{equation*}
is measurable for all open sets \(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}\text{.}\) Hence \(f \circ \varphi\) is a measurable function, by Proposition 5.1.4.
